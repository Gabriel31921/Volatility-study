Volatility is a well-studied phenomenon in financial markets. With the emergence of cryptocurrencies, particularly Bitcoin, unique questions about volatility behaviour arise, requiring focused study. This paper examines Bitcoin's volatility from an observational standpoint, emphasizing volatility clustering-its persistence, market impacts, and opportunities for financial agents. Using Bitcoin data from 2018-08-18 to 2024-11-25, historical volatility is calculated across multiple time frames (1h to 1d) with windows up to 90 days of data. Fundamental techniques such as stationarity testing and autocorrelation analysis are combined with advanced probabilistic modelling through Markov chains, constructed around the 20th and 80th percentiles of volatility. The analysis reveals strong evidence of clustering, particularly in shorter time frames, allowing for statistically significant predictions of market movements. Evidence is find that clustering holds even for larger periods of time, but the low volatility periods lose the tie with the low risk periods, as the standard deviation of future moves grows more in the low volatility percentiles than in the high volatility ones. These findings hold potential for informing hedging strategies, position management, and arbitrage. By highlighting both the limits and opportunities of clustering-based predictability. This study contributes a novel perspective to the understanding of Bitcoin's volatility dynamics and its implications for financial decision-making.
